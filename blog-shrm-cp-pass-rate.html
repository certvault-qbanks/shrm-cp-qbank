SHRM-CP Pass Rate: What the Numbers Mean and How to Beat Them

SHRM's published SHRM-CP pass rates have held between 67% and 71%. Here's what that means, how scoring works, and how to prepare so you're in the passing group.

8 min read • September 2026

In this article

  1. The published pass rates
  2. How scoring works
  3. Why candidates fall short
  4. What strong candidates do
  5. If you don't pass

SHRM publishes global pass rates after each testing window. The SHRM-CP numbers have been steady: most candidates pass, but a meaningful share don't. Knowing where the risk comes from helps you prepare with purpose.

The published pass rates

Exam periodSHRM-CP pass rateSHRM-SCP pass rate
May – July 202568%50%
Dec. 2024 – Feb. 202567%51%
May – July 202471%56%
Dec. 2023 – Feb. 202470%53%
May – July 202371%54%

Across these five windows, the SHRM-CP pass rate has stayed between 67% and 71%. Put another way, roughly three in ten candidates don't pass. The two most recent windows in the table are also the lowest. Those rates are global, covering candidates of every experience level and country.

How scoring works

SHRM reports results as scaled scores from 120 to 200, and every passing candidate receives 200. Performance is measured against a predetermined standard, not against other test takers. There's no curve, and there's no published "percent correct" cutoff, because scaling adjusts for differences in difficulty between exam forms.

Two practical takeaways:

  • Your score report won't tell you by how much you passed. Aim well above the line in practice rather than trying to scrape by.
  • The 24 field-test items don't count, but you can't identify them. A strange or unfamiliar question may be one of them. Answer it and move on.

Why candidates fall short

SHRM doesn't publish reasons for failure, but the exam's design points to the usual risk areas:

  1. Underpreparing for judgment questions. SJIs make up 40% of the exam. Candidates who only read content often find their knowledge doesn't translate into picking the best option.
  2. Relying on "how we do it at work." Workplace habits aren't always best practice. The exam's answers reflect what experienced HR professionals agree is most effective.
  3. Gaps outside their specialty. A recruiter may know talent acquisition cold but be shaky on total rewards, labor relations, or global HR.
  4. Studying an outdated framework. The 2026 BASK changed the behavioral competencies and added AI content.
  5. Running short on time. Sections don't share time, and the break doesn't pause the clock.

What strong candidates do

  • Start with a diagnostic and study their weakest domains first, not their favorites.
  • Review every practice question and can explain why each wrong option is wrong.
  • Take at least two full-length timed exams before test day.
  • Study from the current BASK and check that their materials reflect it.
  • Keep a topic-level error log and use it to guide their final two weeks.

If you don't pass

It's more common than people admit, and it's fixable. Use your score report to see where you were weakest, then register for a future window. You'll pay the exam fee again, so check the current fees and deadlines and review the retake policy in the SHRM Certification Handbook.

For your second attempt, change the method, not just the hours. Most retakers benefit from far more scenario practice and a structured study plan.

Source: SHRM Exam Options and Fees (Exam Statistics), accessed September 2026.
