SHRM-CP vs PHR: Which HR Certification Should You Get?

A side-by-side look at the SHRM-CP and HRCI's PHR: what each exam tests, eligibility, format, and how to decide which one fits your HR career.

9 min read • September 2026

  2. Side-by-side comparison

  5. Should you get both?

The SHRM-CP and the PHR are the two best-known credentials for HR professionals at the operational level. Both are respected, and both can strengthen a resume. They come from different organizations and test in different ways, so the better choice depends on where you are and where you're headed.

The short version

  • SHRM-CP is offered by SHRM and built on the SHRM BASK. It tests both HR knowledge and behavioral competencies, with a large share of situational judgment questions.
  • PHR is offered by HR Certification Institute (HRCI). It has traditionally focused more on technical and operational HR knowledge, with a strong U.S. law and compliance component.

How the exams feel different

The biggest practical difference is the situational judgment component. On the SHRM-CP, 54 of 134 questions are scenarios where several responses could work and you choose the best one. That rewards practical HR judgment and familiarity with how SHRM frames good practice.

Candidates who think in terms of "What's the rule?" sometimes find a knowledge-heavy exam more comfortable. Candidates who think in terms of "What would I actually do here?" often find the SHRM-CP format more natural. Neither is easier across the board.

Which one should you choose?

The SHRM-CP may fit better if you:

  • Are early in your HR career or transitioning from another field.
  • Want a credential that emphasizes both behavior and knowledge.
  • Work in an organization or region where SHRM membership and chapters are active.
  • Do well with scenario-based reasoning.

The PHR may fit better if you:

  • Already meet HRCI's experience requirements.
  • Work in a compliance-heavy U.S. HR role.
  • See it listed more often in job postings for roles you want.
  • Prefer a more knowledge-focused exam.

A simple tie-breaker

Search 20 job postings for the role you want next, in your city. Count which credential appears more often. Employers frequently list both as acceptable, but local patterns are the most useful signal you'll find.

Should you get both?

Some HR professionals hold both, but most people get more value from earning one, building experience, and later moving to the senior level (SHRM-SCP or SPHR). Each credential has its own recertification requirements, so holding two means maintaining two.
